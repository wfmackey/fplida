### i\_etp\_below\_RBL

<br>**Form**:  
Individual Tax Return  
<br>**Section**:  
Income  
<br>**Question**:  
Eligible termination payment: assessable amount (other than excessive
component)  
<br>**Label**:  
Taxable component of eligible termination payments below the reasonable
benefit limit (RBL)  
<br>**General Notes**:  
The employee termination payment amount below the reasonable benefit
limit.  

<br>**Latest or Current Conditions**:  
Latest year: 2006-07. This variable refers to an eligible termination
payment (ETP), which includes:  
- a lump sum payment from your employer when the taxpayer retired or
ceased employment  
- a lump sum payment from a superannuation fund, approved deposit fund,
retirement savings account or life insurance company  
- a similar payment to those above, paid as the beneficiary of a person
who has died. This is called a ‘death benefit ETP’. However, if the
payments were made to the taxpayer as the trustee of a deceased estate
they must be shown on the trust tax return, not on your personal tax
return  
- a payment from the Tax Office of amounts collected from an employer
either by way of Superannuation Guarantee Charge payments or payments to
the Superannuation Holding Accounts Special Account.  
Lump sum payment from employer includes:  
- a payment in lieu of notice or a golden handshake  
- a payment for unused sick leave or unused rostered days off  
- compensation for loss of a job or wrongful dismissal  
- a redundancy payment or an approved early retirement scheme payment
that exceeded the tax free threshold  
- a payment received because of invalidity  
- a similar payment paid to the trustee of a deceased estate after the
death of an employee.   An ETP also includes a payment from a
superannuation fund, an approved deposit fund or a life insurance
company such as:  
- payments, other than a pension or annuity, from a superannuation
fund  
- payments received when a pension or annuity is changed into a lump
sum  
- payments made to the trustee of a deceased estate.  
  
Lump sum payment received on termination of foreign employment and from
a non-resident superannuation fund is recorded at item 19: Foreign
source income and foreign assets or property (see
isn\_fsi\_assessable\_inc).  
  
The taxpayer may have the option to roll-over their ETP. This means
transferring all or part of their ETP to a complying superannuation fund
or an approved deposit fund or buying an annuity. This is not recorded
under i\_etp.  
  
A reasonable benefit limit (RBL) is the maximum amount of superannuation
benefit that is taxed at concessional (or reduced) rates. Anything above
the RBL is taxed at the marginal tax rate.  
   

<br>**Latest or Current Calculations**:  
Latest year 2006-07: the eligible termination payment recorded under
this variable is the sum of:  
- 5% of the total of ‘Concessional Component’ and ‘Pre July 1983
Component’  
- untaxed element of the ‘Post June 1983 Component’  
- taxed element of the ‘Post June 1983 Component’,  
that is below the RBL and has not been rolled-over.  
   

<br>**Earliest Conditions**:  
For 1994-95, this variable refers to the taxable component below the
Reasonable Benefit Limit (RBL) of an eligible termination payment (ETP).
ETP is a lump sum paid to the taxpayer by their employer when they
retire or finish a job, such as:  
- a payment in lieu of notice or a golden handshake  
- a payment for unused sick leave or unused rostered days off  
- compensation for loss of a job or wrongful dismissal  
- a redundancy payment or an approved early retirement scheme payment
that exceeded the tax free threshold  
- a payment received because of invalidity  
- a similar payment paid to the trustee of a deceased estate after the
death of an employee.   An ETP also includes a payments from a
superannuation fund, an approved deposit fund or a life insurance
company such as:  
- payments, other than a pension or annuity, from a superannuation
fund  
- payments received when a pension or annuity is changed into a lump
sum  
- payments made to the trustee of a deceased estate.  
  
The taxpayer may have the option to roll-over their ETP. This means
transferring all or part of their ETP to a complying superannuation fund
or an approved deposit fund or buying an annuity. This is not recorded
under i\_etp.

A reasonable benefit limit (RBL) is the maximum amount of superannuation
benefit that is taxed at concessional (or reduced) rates. Anything above
the RBL is taxed at the marginal tax rate.  

<br>**Earliest Calculations**:  
For 1994-95, the eligible termination payment recorded under this
variable is the sum of:  
- 5% of the total of ‘Concessional Component’ and ‘Pre July 1983
Component’  
- untaxed element of the ‘Post June 1983 Component’  
- taxed element of the ‘Post June 1983 Component’,  
that is below the RBL and has not been rolled-over.

<br>**Event time line: Significance, Type of change(s), Description of
change(s)**:  

**1995**: Major; Introduced; New variable.  
Major; Introduced; Total eligible termination payment \[i\_etp\] split
into taxable amount other than excessive componant
[i\_etp\_below\_RBL](#i_etp_below_rbl) and excessive componant
\[i\_etp\_excess\].

**1996**: Minor; Component; ETP also includes a payment from the
Superannuation Holding Accounts Reserve and the shortfall component of a
superannuation guarantee charge.

Lump sum on termination of foreign employment or from a non-resident
superannuation fund is also recorded at Question 15 General net foreign
source income (see is\_fsi\_empl\_pension).

If the taxpayer is aged 55 or over and have a post-June 1983 component
shown on their STP, this portion of the ETP is taxed at a lower rate up
to a lifetime limit called the Low Rate Threshold. This limit is indexed
each year and one it has been used up, it cannot be used again in future
years except for an extra amount added for annual indexation.

**1997**: Minor; Component; A similar payment paid to the taxpayer as a
trustee of a deceased estate after the death of an employee must record
the payment under the Trust Tax Return form instead.

To qualify for the Low Rate Threshold the taxpayer must have been 55
years or older when they received payments since 1 July 1988 of their
post-June 1983 component.

**1998**: Minor; Component; ETP also includes a payment from the sale of
an active asset of a small business which would otherwise be an
assessable capital gain - called the CGT exempt component.

The untaxed element of the post-June 1983 component will be taxed at 30%
if the taxpayer is under 55 years of age. If the taxpayer is above 55
years of age, the rate under the threshold (see 1998 TaxPack) will be
15% but above the threshold, the excess will be taxed at 30%. Similarly,
the taxed element of the post-June 1983 component will be taxed at 20%
if the taxpayer is under 55 years of age. If the taxpayer is above 55
years of age, the rate under the threshold (see 1998 TaxPack) will be 0%
but above the threshold, the excess will be taxed at 15%.

Any excessive component is taxed at the highest marginal rate plus
Medicare levy rate.

**1999**: Minor; Component; CGT exempt component is non-taxable and is
not included in the calculation of i\_etp.

**2008**: Major; Discontinued; Variable discontinued.
