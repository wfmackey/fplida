### i\_div\_frank

<br>**Form**:

<br>**Section**:

<br>**Question**:

<br>**Label**:

<br>**General Notes**:  
Income from dividends: franked amount.  
  
Dividends paid to shareholders by Australian resident companies are
taxed under a system known as ‘imputation’. It is called an imputation
system because the tax paid by a company may be imputed or attributed to
the shareholders. The tax paid by the company is allocated to
shareholders by way of franking credits attached to the dividends they
receive.  
  
The basis of the system is that if a company pays or credits you with
dividends which have been franked, you may be entitled to a franking tax
offset for the tax the company has paid on its income. The franking tax
offset will cover or partly cover the tax payable on the dividends.  
  
Dividends can be fully franked (meaning that the whole amount of the
dividend carries a franking credit) or partly franked (meaning that the
dividend has a franked amount and an unfranked amount). The dividend
statement or distribution statement you receive from the company paying
the franked dividend must state the amount of the franking credit and
the amounts of the franked and unfranked parts of the dividend.  
  
Franked dividends have a franking/imputation credit attached to them.  

  

<br>**Latest or Current Conditions**:  
This variable refers to franked amounts on dividends and distributions
that were paid by Australian companies to taxpayers who owned shares.
These include:  
- dividends applied under a dividend reinvestment plan  
- dividends that were dealt with on your behalf  
- bonus shares that qualify as dividends.  
  
Dividends include distributions by a corporate limited partnership or
paid by a corporate unit trust, public trading trust, or listed
investment company. They can also include:  
- earnings received, or were credited with, on a non-share equity
interest  
- amounts received from, or were credited by, a private company as a
shareholder or an associate of a shareholder in the form of payments,
loans or debts forgiven (these are generally unfranked dividends)  
- amounts received from, or were credited by, the trustee of a trust
estate in the form of payments, loans or debts forgiven where a private
company in which they were a shareholder, or an associate of a
shareholder, had an unpaid present entitlement (or was going to have
such an entitlement by a certain time) from the trust (these are
generally unfranked dividends).  
  
The dividend amounts recorded under this variable is the dividends that
have a franking credit attached to them. Franking credits are amounts of
tax paid by the company that are allocated to the taxpayer’s dividend or
distributions. This franking credit can be claimed as a tax credit.
However, taxpayer might not be entitled to claim the franking credits
if:  
- within 45 days of buying the shares (90 days for certain preference
shares), they either sold them or entered into an arrangement to reduce
the risk of making a loss on them (Holding period rule)  
- they were under an obligation to make, or were likely to make, a
related payment (Related payments rule), or  
- they received a dividend as a result of a dividend washing arrangement
(Dividend washing integrity rule).  
  
If there are any shares in joint names, then only the proportion of
dividends is shown.   If the taxpayer:  
- received a distribution from a partnership or trust, see Supplement
section question: Partnerships and trusts (is\_pt\_net\_dist\_pp or
is\_pt\_net\_dist\_npp) (for example, in tax return form 2017-18, see
question 13)  
- carried on a business of trading in shares and received income or
loss, see Supplement section question: Net income or loss from business
(is\_bus\_npp) (in tax return form 2017-18, see question 15)  
- sold, redeemed, cancelled or otherwise disposed of shares during the
year (but did not carry on a business of trading in shares), see
Supplement section question: Capital gains (is\_cg\_tot\_current) (in
tax return form 2017-18, see question 18)  
- received dividends from a foreign company, see Supplement section
question: Foreign source income and foreign assets or property
(is\_fsi\_other\_foreign) (in tax return form 2017-18, see question
20)  
- received dividends from a New Zealand company with Australian franking
credits attached, see Supplement section question: Foreign source income
and foreign assets or property (is\_frank\_cr\_nz\_coy) (in tax return
form 2017-18, see question 20)  
- received dividends or a distribution on which family trust
distribution tax had been paid, see Supplement section question: Amount
on which family trust distribution tax has been paid
(as\_family\_trust\_inc\_txd) (in tax return form 2017-18, see question
A5).  
   

<br>**Latest or Current Calculations**:  
Add up all franked dividend amounts from dividend statements. Do not
include any TFN amounts.   

<br>**Earliest Conditions**:  
For 1990-91, the variable refers to franked amounts on dividends where
the taxpayer was a shareholder in a company or held units in a corporate
unit trust or a public trading trust. A corporate unit trusts are public
trusts that are created as part of a reorganisation of a company. Public
trading trusts are commercial enterprises organised as trusts. Both
corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts are taxed as companies.
Most public investment trusts are not corporate unit trusts or public
trading trusts.  
  
The dividends reported excludes:  
- any dividends received by a partnership or trust, which is included at
Question 10: Partnership and trusts (is\_pt\_net\_dist\_npp or
is\_pt\_net\_dist\_pp).  
- any foreign source dividends, which is included at Question 16:
Attributed foreign income.    
Dividend imputation  
From 1 July 1987, dividends paid to Australian shareholders by
Australian companies, corporate unit trusts and public trading trusts
have been taxed under the imputation system. Under this system, any
dividends paid by resident companies that have paid Australian company
taxes carry an imputation credit. These dividends are called franked
dividends. The imputation credit is 39/61 times the franked amount of
the dividend. If the taxpayer receives a franked dividend and is an
Australian resident that are entitled to a rebate, this rebate will
reduce the tax paid. However, the rebate cannot be refunded or used to
reduce the Medicare levy. The Medicare levy will be calculated based on
taxable income, which includes the amount of imputation credit.  
  
Non-resident individuals  
If the taxpayer is a non-resident, the franked dividends they receive
are exempt from withholding tax and imputation credit cannot be
refunded. Unfranked dividends are subject to withholding tax. They do
not show their dividend income on the tax return.  
  
Expenses associated with earning dividends  
These are claimed under Question 27: Interested and dividend deductions
(d\_interest\_div).  
   

<br>**Earliest Calculations**:  
For 1990-91, calculate the total franked amounts from each dividend.

**1992**: Moderate; Component; Dividend can also be partly franked.  
‘TFN amounts deducted’ on dividends  
From 1 July 1991, if taxpayers quoted their tax file number to
investment bodies for shares held that pay unfranked dividends, they
would avoid having any tax deducted on their unfranked dividends. This
tax deducted is called the ‘TFN amounts deducted’. This is calculated as
the highest rate of tax plus Medicare levy (a combined total of 48.25%).
The amount of ‘Unfranked amount’ in the tax return form should be the
unfranked dividends received before any net ‘TFN amounts deducted’ was
deducted.

**1994**: Moderate; Component; Non-resident individuals need to show any
unfranked or partially franked dividends from which dividend withholding
tax has not been deducted. If the individual received partially franked
dividends, the unfranked portion is subject to tax at the dividend
withholding rate of 30%. If they are a residents of country which has
double tax agreement with Australian, the dividend withholding tax rate
is generally reduced to 15%.

‘TFN amounts deducted’ has a combined total of 48.4%.

**1996**: Minor; Component; TFN amounts deducted’ has a combined total
of 48.5%.

**1997**: Minor; Component; Do not show cents. ‘TFN amounts deducted’
has a combined total of 48.7%.

**1998**: Major; Component; Assessable dividends include those paid
directly, applied under a dividend reinvestment plan, dividends which
are otherwise dealt on the taxpayer’s behalf, and bonus shares which
qualify as dividends.

Under proposed legislation, the taxpayer may not entitled to claim the
franking credits if:  
- within 45 days of buying the shares (90 days for certain preference
shares), on or after 1 July 1997, they either sold them or entered into
an arrangement to reduce the risk of making a loss on them (Holding
period rule)  
- they were under an obligation to make, or were likely to make, a
related payment, on arrangements entered onto on or after 13 May 1997

If the taxpayer does not meet the holding period rule or the related

If taxpayer sold shares during the year they may need to report any

‘TFN amounts deducted’ has a combined total of 48.5%.
