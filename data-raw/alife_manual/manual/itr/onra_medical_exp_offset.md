### onra\_medical\_exp\_offset

<br>**Form**:  
Individual Tax Return  
<br>**Section**:  
Supplementary section: Tax offsets  
<br>**Question**:  
Total net medical expenses (for disability aids, attendant care or aged
care from 2016).   
<br>**Label**:  
Medical expenses tax offset - available amount.   
<br>**General Notes**:  
The Net Medical Expenses Tax Offset is a tax offset for net medical
expenses incurred by the taxpayer. Introduced in 1997, the tax offset is
20% of net medical expenses over $1250.  
  
Between 1997-2015: the question refers to the total net medical
expenses. Between 2016-2019: the question refers to total net medical
expenses for disability aids, attendant care or aged care.  
  
The question was removed in income year 2020.  
  

<br>**Latest or Current Conditions**:  
Latest year: 2018-19. This variable refers to a tax offset on net
medical expenses for disability aids, attendant care or aged care. This
tax offset is income tested and is calculated by the Australian Taxation
Office.  

<br>**Latest or Current Calculations**:  
(a) Add up disability aids, attendant care or aged care expenses.  
(b) Add up refunds for these expenses which the taxpayer or any other
person has received or are entitled to receive.  
(c) Take (b) away from (a). This amount is the total net medical
expenses.  
(d) The tax offset is 20% of net medical expenses over $1250.  
   

<br>**Earliest Conditions**:  
For 1996-97, this variable refers to the 20 per cent tax rebate on net
medical expenses over $1250. The medical expenses must be for the
taxpayer, their spouse, any maintained children, student, or
child-housekeeper or an invalid relative, parent or spouse’s parent. For
the full list of claimable medical expenses see TaxPack 1997.   

<br>**Earliest Calculations**:  
For 1996-97,   (a) Add up all medical expenses net total refunds
received or are entitled to receive.  
(b) Take $1250 away from (a) and divide the remaining amount by 5.  
The amount at (b) is onra\_medical\_exp\_offset.

<br>**Event time line: Significance, Type of change(s), Description of
change(s)**:  

**1997**: Major; Introduced; New variable.

**1998**: Minor; Question; Question changed from “Medical expenses” to
“Rebate on net medical expenses over $1250”. The definition does not
change.

**2001**: Minor; Question; Question changed from “Rebate on net medical
expenses over $1250” to “20% tax offset on net medical expenses over
$1250”.

**2003**: Moderate; Question; Question changed from “20% tax offset on
net medical expenses over $1250” to “20% tax offset on net medical
expenses over the threshold amount”. At the time of printing, the
Parliament was considering changes to the law to increase the threshold
from $1,250 to $1,500. Taxpayer was still required to complete this
question using a threshold of $1,250 but this amount was adjusted by the
ATO after the law came into effect.

**2004**: Major; Component; Threshold on net medical expenses increased
from $1,250 to $1,500.

**2011**: Major; Component; Threshold on net medical expenses increased
from $1,500 to $2,000.

**2012**: Moderate; Component; Threshold on net medical expenses
increased from $2,000 to $2,060.

**2013**: Major; Question/Component; Major; Question; Question changed
from “20% tax offset on net medical expenses over the threshold amount”
to “Total net medical expenses”.  
  
Major; Calculation; Tax offset is now calculated by ATO depending on
taxpayer’s family status and ATI. For more information on this tax
offset calculation see
<https://www.ato.gov.au/Individuals/Tax-Return/2013/Supplementary-tax-return/Tax-offset-questions-T4-T11/T6---Total-net-medical-expenses-2013/>.  
Moderate; Legislative; Income tests were introduced from 1 July 2012,
with the rebate rate based on the taxpayers’ combined adjusted taxable
income.

**2014**: Major; Component; Legislation introduced changing the
eligibility for the net medical expenses tax offset. The taxpayer is
eligible to received this tax offset if they either received this offset
in their 2012–13 income tax assessment; or paid for medical expenses
relating to disability aids, attendant care or aged care.

**2016**: Major; Question; Question changed from “Total net medical
expenses” to “Total net medical expenses for disability aids, attendant
care or aged care”.

**2019**: Major; Discontinued; Tax offset abolished as of 1 July 2019.
