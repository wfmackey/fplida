### is\_cg\_net

<br>**Form**:  
Individual Tax Return  
<br>**Section**:  
Supplementary section: Income  
<br>**Question**:  
Capital gain: Net Capital gain  
<br>**Label**:  
Net capital gains  
<br>**General Notes**:  
Net capital gain calculated as the taxpayers total capital gains less
entitled discounts, concessions and current capital losses and unapplied
net capital losses brought forward from prior income years.  
  
The “cost base” of an asset forms a fundamental step in determining
whether a taxpayer has derived a capital gain or incurred a capital loss
following the disposal of an asset. In its simplest form a taxable gain
is calculated as the difference between the amount you receive upon
disposal (“disposal consideration”) and the cost to you of the asset
(“cost base”), indexed for inflation. Similarly a capital loss is
calculated as the difference between the amount you paid (“cost base”)
and the amount you receive upon disposal (“disposal consideration”).  
  
In determining the amount of a capital gain or capital loss, section
160Z of the Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (“the 1936 Act”) requires the
taxpayer to compare the consideration received on the disposal of an
asset with the asset’s cost base. Generally, a capital gain will arise
where the disposal consideration exceeds the “cost base” (or “indexed
cost base” where the asset was held for greater than 12 months).
Alternatively, a capital loss will arise where the “reduced cost base”
exceeds the disposal consideration.    

<br>**Latest or Current Conditions**:  
This variable refers to net capital gain made by the taxpayer. The
taxpayer made a capital gain, or capital loss if a capital gains tax
(CGT) event happened in the income year. A capital gain occurred if the
amount of money and property received, or were entitled to receive, from
the CGT event was more than the cost base of the asset and tax is paid
on the capital gain. A capital loss occurred if the amount of money and
property received, or were entitled to receive, from the CGT event was
less than the reduced cost base of the asset.  
Examples of common CGT events are if the taxpayer sold or gave away a
CGT asset, such as:  
- real estate, including family home, a holiday home, investment
property, hobby farm or vacant block of land  
- shares  
- units in a unit trust or managed investment fund  
- forestry managed investment scheme interests (as a subsequent
participant)  
- collectables, for example, jewellery  
- personal use assets.  
  
Other CGT events include:  
- an owned asset was lost or destroyed  
- an amount received for entering into an agreement, for example, you
agreed not to work in a particular industry for a set period of time / -
entering into a conservation covenant over land that they owned  
- receiving a non-assessable payment from a trust or company.  
  
Exceptions:  
This does not include any “listed investment company capital gain
amount” included in a dividend paid by a listed investment company. This
is reported under Question 8: Dividend deductions (d\_div). If the
taxpayer is a Norfolk Island resident, CGT may apply to assets acquired
after 23 October 2015. The taxpayer cannot deduct a capital loss from
their assessible income.  
Generally, a capital gain or loss is disregarded if it relates to:  
- assets acquired before 20 September 1985  
- cars, motorcycles and similar vehicles  
- compensation received for personal injury  
- disposal of main residence  
- collectables, for example an antique or jewellery, which was acquired
for $500 or less  
- the exchange of shares or units owned in a company or trust under a
takeover, if certain conditions were met  
- shares in a company or interests in a trust where there had been a
demerger and certain conditions had been met  
- disposal of an asset to which the small business 15-year exemption
applies  
- disposal of certain investments by a venture capital limited
partnership, an early stage venture capital limited partnership, or an
Australian venture capital fund of funds  
- disposal of shares in a pooled development fund  
- personal use assets, such as boats, furniture, electrical goods and
household items used or kept mainly for personal use or enjoyment. If it
was acquired for more than $10,000, only capital losses is disregarded.
If it was for $10,000 or less, then both capital gains and capital
losses is disregarded.  
- transfer of an asset where the Small business restructure roll-over is
available (gains or losses are deferred until the asset is disposed of).
   

<br>**Latest or Current Calculations**:  
Step 1: Read the publication that is relevant to your circumstances and
work out the amount of your capital gain or capital loss for each CGT
event that occurred, and the amount of your capital gains from a trust
(including a managed fund) for the income year.  
Step 2: Add up all your capital gains for the income year (except those
that are disregarded) to work out your total current year capital gains.
Do not apply yet capital losses, any CGT discounts or the small business
concessions (other than the 15-year exemption). Write this amount at H
item 18 (isn\_cg\_tot\_current).  
Step 3: Work out your net capital gain or net capital loss. This is the
amount remaining after applying to your current year capital gains
whichever of the following items are relevant to you (in the order
listed): / - capital losses in the income year / - unapplied net capital
losses from earlier years / - any CGT discounts / - the small business
50% active asset reduction / - the small business retirement exemption
or rollover. / / If the total amount remaining is positive or zero,
write it at A item 18. If you have a negative amount, do not put
anything at A. You have net capital losses to carry forward to later
income years. Write the amount at V item 18
(isn\_cg\_loss\_carry\_fwd).  
   

<br>**Earliest Conditions**:  
For 1990-91, this variable refers to the net capital gain from assets
sold or disposed of, or any other capital amounts received during the
financial year. This also includes forfeited deposit or a capital gain
distributed to a beneficiary of a trust. Net capital gain may be subject
to capital gain tax.  
  
Capital gains tax may apply if the taxpayer sold or disposed of:  
- listed personal use assets, these includes assets owned for personal
use and enjoyment and that cost more than $100 to buy.  
- non listed personal-use assets, these include any other assets that
was owned for personal use and enjoyment and which was disposed of for
more than $5000 each.   - other assets, for example shares or units in
unit trusts, options or rights to acquire shares or units, real estate
(including holiday home), any other assets.  

Assets not subject to capital gains tax include:  
- most assets acquired before 20 September 1985. However, if any capital
improvements were made to those assets after that date, capital gains
may apply to those improvements.   - in most cases, the taxpayer’s
home  
- motor vehicles designed to carry less than nine passengers or one
tonne  
- motorcycles.  
  
Taxpayer may offset current capital loss during the income year or for
future income years.  
   

<br>**Earliest Calculations**:  
For 1990-91,   For assets held less than 12 months:  
Capital gain = consideration received minus cost base.  
Capital loss = reduced cost base minus consideration received.  

For assets held more than 12 months:  
Capital gain = consideration received minus indexed cost base.  
Capital loss = reduced cost base minus consideration received.  

For non-listed personal-use assets:  
Similarly, but if the cost base or indexed cost base is less than $5000
it is taken to be $5000.  
  
Consideration received: amount received, or are entitled to receive for
disposing of an asset or part of an asset. If the consideration is in
form of property or gift, the consideration is the market value of the
asset.   Cost base: is the total of the cost of acquiring the assets,
capital cost of maintaining a right over or title to the assets, costs
of capital improvements (no repairs), and incidental costs (e.g. costs
of advertising).  
Indexed cost base: applies if the asset was owned for more than 12
months. Multiply the cost base of the asset by it “indexing factor”.  
Reduced cost base: is the total of the cost base less allowable
deductions plus relevant income (any amount that has been or will be
included in assessable income as a result of the disposal of an asset
and relating to income tax deductions that have been or are allowed in
respect of the costs).
